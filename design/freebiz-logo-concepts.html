Freebiz · mark concepts · round two

Five marks, no letterforms

The first attempt put a white letter inside a violet rounded square, which is the single most occupied shape in social media. These five drop the letter entirely and build the mark out of what the product actually does: something valuable falls off a screen and somebody catches it. The wordmark stays as it was — lowercase, with the freebie as the dot on the i.

01

The Catch

Recommended Open form · no container

An open hand under a falling coin

A hook that opens upward, catching a yellow disc mid-drop. The right arm runs taller than the left, so the shape leans and feels like it's moving rather than sitting still. It is not enclosed in anything — which is precisely why it can't be mistaken for an app-store squircle.

  • It says the verb. Freebiz is a catching game, and this is the only concept where the mark shows the act rather than the object.
  • Two elements, two colours. Violet is the platform, yellow is the freebie. The same rule as the wordmark's dot, so the logo teaches the colour system.
  • Animatable. The disc can drop into the cup on app launch and on every win — one asset, one motion, used everywhere.

The Drop

Landscape · screen-shaped

The board, with something falling off the corner

A 16:9 outline — the billboard itself — and a yellow disc breaking away from the top-right corner, half in and half out. The mark is wider than it is tall, so it can never be confused with the square social icons it sits beside on a phone home screen.

  • Literal and instantly legible. People understand "screen" before they understand any abstraction.
  • The corner break is the memorable bit. The disc crossing the frame edge is what makes it a logo instead of a rectangle.
  • Trade-off: outlined rectangles are common in media brands, so it is the safest and least distinctive of the five.

The Tear

Ticket · perforation

One ticket, torn between two people

A single ticket bitten at the top and bottom, with a dotted tear line down the middle: the brand keeps one half, the viewer keeps the other. The yellow disc sits on the viewer's side. It is the only concept that shows the deal — attention going one way, a freebie coming back the other.

  • Nigerian shorthand. Perforated stubs and scratch panels are the everyday material of recharge cards and raffle books.
  • Strong horizontally. Excellent on a header, a banner, or the side of a flier.
  • Trade-off: the dotted tear closes up below about 24px — you'd ship a simplified two-block version for the favicon.

The Ticker

Derived from the z

The strips that carry the codes

Three strokes, built from the last letter of the name. The top strip is yellow because that is where the freebie codes live on the real board; the diagonal is the drop, and the bottom bar is the apply field catching it. It reads as a z, as a lightning bolt, and as a ticker — all three are on-brief.

  • Ties the mark to the interface. The billboard's perimeter strips are the product's most distinctive UI element, and this is that element compressed into a glyph.
  • Excellent at tiny sizes. Three heavy strokes with round caps survive a 16px favicon better than any other option here.
  • Trade-off: a bold z can read as a generic "zap" energy mark unless the yellow-over-violet split is preserved exactly.

The Split

Circular · two halves

Split down the middle, shared

A disc cut by a diagonal gap into a violet half and a yellow half — the platform's side and the viewer's side of the same coin, pulled slightly apart. No outline, no container, no letter. It is the simplest mark here and the one that will still be recognisable when it is three millimetres tall on a sticker.

  • Pure geometry. Nothing to render wrong, nothing to age, no typeface dependency.
  • The gap does the work. The negative diagonal is what makes it read as "shared" rather than as a pie chart.
  • Trade-off: the most abstract of the five. It needs the wordmark beside it for a while before it can stand alone.

Which one to ship

The Catch (01) — it names the verb, it carries the colour rule, it animates for free, and no major platform owns an open upward hook. The Ticker (04) is the strongest backup and the best pure favicon. Everything else in the mockups already works with any of the five: the mark is a single SVG swap.
